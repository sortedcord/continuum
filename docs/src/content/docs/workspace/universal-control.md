---
title: Universal Control (Cross-Device Mouse & Keyboard)
description: Seamlessly control both your Linux computer and Android devices with a single keyboard and mouse.
---

Unlike Screen Mirroring (which streams video into a window) or Sidecar (which turns your tablet into a monitor), **Universal Control** keeps each device running its own native operating system while allowing your single physical mouse and keyboard to travel seamlessly between them.

## User Experience

### 1. Fluid Screen Edge Transition
* Place your Android tablet or phone physically next to your Linux monitor (for example, to the right of your laptop).
* Push your mouse pointer past the right edge of your Linux screen:
  * A subtle glow appears at the edge of the Android screen.
  * The pointer pushes smoothly across the physical gap and emerges directly onto your Android device!
* You are now moving the native Android cursor on the phone/tablet.

### 2. Unified Typing & Shortcuts
* Wherever your mouse cursor is located, your physical desktop keyboard follows automatically:
  * Click into a note-taking app on your Android tablet and type using your laptop's mechanical keyboard.
  * Move the cursor back to your laptop to resume typing in your Linux IDE.
* Standard desktop hotkeys (e.g. `Ctrl+A`, `Ctrl+C`, navigation arrows) work across both platforms.

### 3. Drag-and-Drop Cross-Device Assets
* Click and drag an image or document from your Linux desktop file manager, move the mouse across the screen border into the Android screen, and drop it straight into a mobile app or email composer.

---

## Continuum Augmentations (Beyond Apple)

* **Multi-Device Custom Layouts**: Arrange multiple Android devices around your monitors in any spatial configuration (left, right, top, bottom, or corner-diagonal transitions).
* **Configurable Edge Stickiness**: Prevent accidental cursor jumps when gaming or moving quickly by setting customizable edge activation resistance or requiring a modifier key (e.g. `Ctrl + Edge`).
* **Directional Reverse Flow**: Use an external keyboard/mouse attached to an Android desktop dock (e.g. Samsung DeX) to control your Linux computer in reverse when needed.
